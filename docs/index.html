Too fast
or too slow?

Run your agents too fast and the 5-hour Claude limit runs out before the reset, stopping them mid-task. Too slow, and the window resets with budget you could have used. AI Tempomat helps you find the pace in between.

Claude's usage page tells you how much of the window is gone, but not whether that's too much for the time that's left. AI Tempomat sits in the macOS menu bar and compares how fast you're using the limit with the pace that would use it up exactly at the reset. That pace is 100%.

Above 100%, you're headed for the limit: let the running agents finish and look at their results before starting new ones. Below 100%, budget will be left over, so there's room for more, if there's work worth doing.

What else is in it

Click the reading in the menu bar and the panel opens. Two more views sit behind it.

Expanded view

Every session as a ribbon of tokens per minute, the ones running right now, and where the limit is heading. When the dial jumps, you can see which agent did it.

History

Every 5-hour window, kept. Looking back over a week shows the pattern: which windows used the budget well, where you hit the limit early, and where budget went unused.

Ethical considerations

Don't start agents just so the budget isn't "wasted".

The dial shows when there's room left in the window. That doesn't mean the room has to be filled. Unused budget isn't waste: a window that resets with 20% left used no extra energy at all.

Tokens that get spent do. Every request runs on GPUs in a data center that draw electricity and need cooling, and depending on where and when that power is generated, it adds to carbon emissions and water use. Spent on real work, that can be worth it. Spent to fill a quota, it isn't.

So use the room when there's work waiting that's worth doing, and let the window reset when there isn't. Treat 100% as a ceiling to plan against, not a score to hit.

Where the numbers come from

It runs claude -p "/usage" every two minutes. That's a local command: it doesn't call the model and costs no quota.

Token counts come from Claude Code's own transcripts in ~/.claude, and the history is kept in ~/Library/Application Support/AI Tempomat. Your usage stays on your Mac.

The one thing the app sends is a check for new versions, at launch and once a day: its version numbers and the macOS version, the branch if it's main or multi-profile, and whether it's the first check today, this week, this month or ever. No ID. That's also how the author counts how many people use it. A checkbox in the panel turns it off, and the README lists exactly what's sent.

Try it

You need macOS 14 or newer. Paste this into Terminal:

# clones and builds it in ~/tempomat-ai
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Taremeh/tempomat-ai/main/scripts/install.sh | sh

Then click the gauge in the menu bar and click Sign in in the panel. Your browser opens claude.com: approve there, and the panel shows your limits. If Claude Code isn't installed yet, the panel offers to install it first.

The script installs Apple's command line tools if they're missing, builds the app, puts it in ~/Applications and starts it. When a new version is out, the panel can update it for you. Ideas and bug reports are welcome on GitHub.
